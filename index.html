Mechanics of Deformable Bodies • Jacobs Section 3.2

In Introduction to Cell Mechanics and Mechanobiology (Jacobs, Huang, & Kwon), Figure 3.3 models a network of polymers as a pin-jointed truss system where individual polymer strands behave like elastic springs with stiffness \(k\).

1. Isolation of Pin 4 (Bottom Node):

When a tensile load \(F\) is applied vertically downward, Pin 4 experiences three forces: the applied force \(-F \hat{j}\), and the tension forces from diagonal members 4-1 and 4-2. By symmetry:

Σ Fy = 2 × Tdiag × cos(θ) - F = 0
⇒ Tdiag = F / (2 × cos(θ)) (Tension)
2. Isolation of Pin 2 (Left Node) & The Compression Paradox:

Students often ask: Why is the middle horizontal polymer in compression even though the overall network is pulled in tension?

At Pin 2, both the top spring (1-2) and the bottom spring (4-1) pull upward/downward and rightward toward the central axis:

Fpull, x = T1-2 × sin(θ) + T4-1 × sin(θ) > 0 (directed to the right)

For horizontal equilibrium (Σ Fx = 0):
Fspring 2-3 must push to the left: F2-3 = -2 × Tdiag × sin(θ) = -F × tan(θ)
Because the member pushes outward against its pins, it is in COMPRESSION!
3. Biological Significance:

This simple 5-polymer network illustrates how tension in the extracellular matrix or actin cortex can produce localized compressive loads on internal struts (e.g. microtubules or cross-linking actin filaments), fundamental to the tensegrity architecture of biological cells.
